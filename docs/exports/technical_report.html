Research explorer

Credit risk under economic stress

Prasanna K · M.A. Actuarial Economics, Madras School of Economics Research completed 18 October 2026 · Release candidate

Decision

Restrict both frozen models to historical research and ranking diagnostics; reject their unchanged probabilities for operational loss or capital decisions. The nonlinear challenger improves held-out Brier score, but this does not repair large aggregate underprediction. The validation-based recommendation remains unchanged logistic regression under the predeclared simplicity tolerance. Test results are evidence about that decision, not permission to select again.

Question and scope

Does a model trained on comparatively low-event historical cohorts remain useful when the event rate and population change? This study uses six official Freddie Mac Release 47 annual samples: 300,000 originated loans and 20,151,667 monthly records. Each sample contains 50,000 loans. One eligible snapshot per loan is used. The target is a reported 90+ day delinquency, REO or specified distress exit in the next 12 reporting months, before voluntary payoff. It is not regulatory default, realized financial loss, an Indian underwriting model or IFRS 9 ECL.

The July 2026 release incorporates later revisions. Features obey SQL reporting cutoffs, but immutable historical releases are unavailable. Cohort differences cannot be attributed solely to COVID-19 or any single economic cause. Accepted loans alone do not identify rejected-applicant performance.

Data integrity and outcome flow

All six source ZIP hashes, 12 member CRCs, field counts, key relationships and SQL integrity checks pass. There are no duplicate IDs or supplied pre-HARP-linked loans across the six files. The transitive-link exclusion logic is tested even though no exclusion is needed in these samples. This cannot rule out common borrowers with different undisclosed identifiers.

Two 2015 loan histories have three missing months. Original data are preserved: the fixed six-month snapshot rule and unresolved-follow-up rules handle them. The earlier 2012 feature/label CSV reproduces its original SHA-256 byte for byte.

Fixed methods and validation decision

The original protocol was fixed on 16 October. The implemented methods were hashed and committed before fitting on 18 October. Both families share 16 numeric and four categorical predictors: origination attributes and six-month performance history, with sentinel decoding, calendar age and no future performance. IDs, geography, seller/servicer, disposition and loss fields are excluded. Modification status means observed in the six-month lookback, not lifetime modification history.

Numeric medians, missingness indicators and one-hot categories are fitted only on the applicable training sample. Unknown categories map to all-zero indicators; boosting never treats arbitrary category codes as ordered values. Logistic uses standardization, L2 C=1 and at most 2,000 iterations. Histogram boosting uses learning rate .05, 200 iterations, seven leaves, minimum 200 rows per leaf, L2=10 and no early stopping. Neither uses class weights or target-based sampling.

Exactly four full fits and two adaptation-only intercept adjustments produce six candidates. All full fits converge; logistic iterations are 31 and 27. There is no hyperparameter search. Each family's validation Brier minimum selects unchanged/recalibrated/retrained, with declared numerical ties. The final family rule prefers logistic within .0001 Brier of boosting.

Review allocation, uncertainty and failure cases

Capacity is a fraction of all eligible loans, rounded upward. Frozen probabilities rank loans; SHA-256 ID hashes break ties. No threshold is chosen on test labels. Known recall divides captured known positives by all known positives. Bounds assign unresolved outcomes adversarially inside/outside the selected group; they are identification bounds, not confidence intervals or intervention benefits.

Broad FICO, LTV and original-term diagnostics include paired Brier-difference intervals and Wilson event-rate intervals. Cells below 30 known loans are suppressed; cells with fewer than 30 events carry an explicit instability flag. These descriptive comparisons have no multiplicity-adjusted superiority claim. Protected attributes are unavailable, so no fairness conclusion is possible. Numeric missingness/median shifts and categorical proportions are published against training. Test drift is descriptive and never changes preprocessing.

The bootstrap treats loans as independent after supplied-link checks. Hidden borrower overlap, geographic/calendar dependence and common economic shocks can make its uncertainty too narrow. It is not a confidence interval for a new macro regime. Equal-count calibration bins are descriptive, not underwriting rules.

Use restrictions and reproducibility

The aggregate explorer changes capacity and explicit severity/review-cost assumptions. Probability × outstanding balance × assumed severity is a conditional scenario based on miscalibrated research probabilities. Serious delinquency is not a realized financial loss. The study does not identify the causal effect of reviewing a borrower; there are no achieved avoided-loss or profitability claims.

Full reproduction requires a permitted Freddie Mac account and the six pinned source ZIPs. Raw rows, IDs, feature/label tables, SQL stores, predictions and fitted models remain private. Public evidence contains aggregates and hashes only. The README lists source auditing, preparation, fitting, sealing, evaluation and report commands. Original protocol/method manifests, selection seal and the explicit metadata-repair record preserve the order of scientific decisions.

Operational approval would require a separately authorized, later-cohort study with fresh probability calibration, population validation, intervention economics and governance. That is outside this frozen release; it is not performed by retuning on these tests.
